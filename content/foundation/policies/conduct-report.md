## Summary

This document is for the people who run the process: the Committee Chair and
the panelists. It describes how a concern moves from first report to closed
case.

The process puts the adopted [Code of Conduct](https://apache.org/foundation/policies/conduct.html)
into practice by providing mediation and a process for reviewing formal Code
of Conduct complaints.

It does not make changes to the Code of Conduct. The escalation ladder, the
Recusal rules, the confidentiality commitment, and the treatment of malicious
reports all come from the existing Code of Conduct. In the event of
conflicting information between this document and the Code of Conduct, defer
to the Code of Conduct.

Complaints marked **[OPEN]** are not yet decided and are still under review.

## The guiding principle

The goal is reconciliation, not retribution. Every step should leave the
people involved better able to work together, and nobody should come out of
it feeling that the process was a weapon. When in doubt about how to handle
something, choose the option that de-escalates.

## Roles

**Chair:** Receives all reports, runs mediation, convenes panels, keeps
records, and carries rulings to the President. The Chair facilitates but does
not vote on any ruling [1]. The Chair reports to the VP Community Development.

**Panel Pool:** Volunteer ASF Members who have confirmed they have read the
Code. Pool members see nothing about any case unless they are selected for it.

**Panel:** Five people drawn at random from the pool for a single case.

**President:** Receives rulings and has them carried out. The President
delegates the decision itself to the panel and is not personally responsible
for it.

**Infra:** Carries out technical measures (mailing list removal, account
suspension) when a ruling requires them.

## Eligibility to serve

- Officers and directors are not eligible for the panel pool [2].
- Every volunteer confirms, in writing, that they have read the Code of
  Conduct.
- For any given case, a panelist must step aside if they are a party to it,
  or have a close personal or professional connection to a party, as the
  Code's Recusal section requires [3].

## Process of handling complaints

### Step one: Arrival

Reports arrive at **conduct@apache.org**. This is a private, unarchived alias
which goes to the Chair.

Reports may also arrive by other routes: direct to the President or EVP, or
referred by a PMC that cannot handle a matter itself. The Code also allows the
President to refer a project-specific report back to the relevant PMC [3].
Whatever the route, the case enters the same process once it reaches the
Chair.

### Step two: Upon receipt of report

Once the report reaches the Chair, the Chair takes the following actions:

1. **Acknowledges receipt:** sends an email to the reporter within 48 hours
   of receipt.
2. **Checks scope:** Is this within the Code? Is it a matter for one project,
   which belongs with that PMC first? If it's out of scope, the Chair says so
   and, where possible, points the reporter somewhere useful.
3. **Inquires about resolution:** Checks whether the reporter tried to
   resolve it directly. If not, and if it is safe to do so, the Chair
   encourages the reporter to take this step first. Many reports end here.
4. **Opens a case record** (see [Records](#records)).

The Chair may close a report with no further action when it does not describe
a Code of Conduct violation. That matches level 1 of the Code's ladder [3].
The Chair relays this information to the reporter, with a short explanation.

### Step three: Mediation

Mediation is always offered first. The Chair (or a mediator the Chair
arranges) sets up a conversation between the people involved, aimed at the
actual issue rather than at who was at fault.

- Participation is voluntary. Neither side is penalized for declining, but if
  mediation is declined or fails, the case may go to a panel.
- Mediation is confidential. What is said there is not passed to a panel as
  evidence, so that people can speak frankly.
- A mediated agreement closes the case. For example, this can come in the
  form of a clarification, an apology, or a change in how the parties
  interact.

The outcome of mediation will be recorded on the original report.

### Step four: Panel session

When a case goes to a panel:

1. The Chair draws names at random from the pool.
2. The Chair contacts each name privately, gives only the names of the
   parties, and asks whether they have a conflict. Anyone with a conflict, or
   who is unavailable, is replaced by the next random draw.
3. Five panelists who confirm no conflict are seated. They then receive the
   case material.

Panelists who are not seated learn nothing beyond the parties' names, and are
asked to keep even those confidential.

#### Gathering the facts

- Panelists receive the report and any supporting material from the Chair.
- Panelists speak privately, by video call, with the person who raised the
  concern and with the person it concerns. Each is told enough about the
  concern to respond to it.
- Panelists may ask either party for more information, and may speak with
  witnesses.
- The Chair attends to keep the process on track, but does not argue for
  either side.

#### Deliberation

The panel answers the question, "Did a Code of Conduct violation take place?"
A vote is by simple majority of panelists.

- **If no:** The outcome is Level 1 of the Code's ladder, "No Action." The
  panel may still require a note to clear up a misunderstanding.
- **If yes:** The Chair then tells the panel whether the person has any prior
  findings, and the panel chooses the level from the Code's ladder:
  Correction, Warning, Temporary Ban, or Permanent Ban [3].

Prior history is held back until after the finding, so it cannot shape
consensus on confirmation of a violation, only how the response is set.

#### Malicious or bad-faith reports

The Code treats knowingly false or malicious reports, and repeated misuse of
the process, as violations in their own right [3]. If the panel concludes a
report was made in bad faith, it may say so in its ruling, and the bad-faith
report can be handled as a separate case.

### Step five: After the ruling

1. The Chair writes up the ruling: the finding, the level, the specific
   consequences, and their duration.
2. The Chair sends it to the President, who has it carried out, including any
   request to Infra.
3. The Chair informs both parties of the outcome, privately.
4. Where the person is an ASF Member, any action affecting membership must
   follow ASF Bylaw 4.7, which requires a vote of the Membership. In the
   meantime, the ruling may limit participation in specific channels or
   events [3].

## Records

Records exist so that later panels can be told about prior findings, and so
the process can be reviewed. They are not a public register.

**Record keeper:** The Chair. Panelists see only their own case, and only
while it is open. **[OPEN]** Whether the President also has access.

**Location:** A restricted location only the Chair can read, for example a
private repository or a directory under Foundation control.

**Contents of a case record:**

- Case ID, dates opened and closed.
- Parties.
- Summary of the concern.
- Stage reached (closed at intake, mediated, or ruled).
- Outcome and ladder level, if any.
- Consequences and their end dates.
- Panelist names, if a panel session occurred.

**Case records do not contain:**

- Notes from mediation sessions, beyond the outcome.

**How long records are kept:** **[OPEN]** Options include:

- deleting records of No Action outcomes after a set period
- keeping findings of a violation for longer, so later panels can see an
  ongoing pattern

**Reporting:** The Chair reports anonymized totals (cases opened, closed, by
stage and outcome) through the ComDev report to the Board. No names or
identifying details will be included in any public reports.
