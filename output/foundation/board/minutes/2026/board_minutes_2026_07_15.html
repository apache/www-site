 7. Special Orders

    A. Create the Responsible AI Executive Committee

       WHEREAS, the Board of Directors deems it to be in the best
       interests of the Foundation and consistent with the
       Foundation's purpose to appoint an officer and create an
       executive committee responsible for establishing, promoting,
       and coordinating governance policies, models, agents, and best
       practices for AI use within the ASF.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the office of "Vice
       President, Responsible AI" be and hereby is created, the person
       holding such office to serve at the direction of the Board of
       Directors, and to have responsibility for establishing,
       promoting, and coordinating governance policies, models,
       agents, and best practices for AI use within the ASF.

       BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that Jeff Genender be and hereby is
       appointed to the office of Vice President, Responsible AI, to
       serve in accordance with and subject to the direction of the
       Board of Directors until death, resignation, retirement,
       removal or disqualification, or until a successor is appointed.

       BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the "Responsible AI Committee" be
       and hereby is created, the members of which to serve at the
       direction of the Board of Directors, and to have responsibility
       to advise the Vice President, Responsible AI on establishing,
       promoting, and coordinating governance policies, models,
       agents, and best practices for AI use within the ASF.

Attachment CJ: Report from the Apache Zeppelin Project  [Jongyoul Lee]

## Description:
The mission of Zeppelin is the creation and maintenance of software
related to a web-based notebook that enables interactive data
analytics

## Project Status:
Current project status: Ongoing with moderate activity.

Issues for the board: Apache Zeppelin 0.12.1 has been released. The PMC is
reviewing newly reported security issues through the private security process.
The release is done, but we have not yet notified the reporters; that
follow-up is still pending.

## Membership Data:
Apache Zeppelin was founded 2016-05-18 (10 years ago).

There are currently 27 committers and 13 PMC members in this project. The
Committer-to-PMC ratio is roughly 7:4.

Community changes, past quarter:
- No new PMC members. Last addition was Cheng Pan on 2024-08-25.
- No new committers. Last committer additions were ChanHo Lee and Gyeongtae
  Park on 2025-08-20.

## Project Activity:
Apache Zeppelin 0.12.1 was released during this period, moving the project
forward after the 0.12.0 release line.

The community focused less on adding broad new surface area and more on making
the codebase easier to maintain, safer to ship, and more approachable for
future contributors.

On the UI side, the modernization effort continued by moving the Angular UI to
a current stack, improving the React integration path, and fixing user-visible
issues around note switching, paragraph cloning, editor focus, and table
result copy support. This work helps reduce the gap between the legacy UI and
the newer UI.

On the runtime and interpreter side, the community continued pruning stale
integrations and updating supported ones. Deprecated Flink shims were removed
while newer Flink support was added, and outdated Alluxio and R/SparkR
interpreter paths were dropped. This keeps the supported surface clearer for
both users and contributors.

Security and maintainability also received attention. The project added
SECURITY.md and THREAT_MODEL.md, followed up on threat-model questions, and
made hardening changes around REST/WebSocket handling, path validation, LDAP
escaping, and dependency updates.

## Community Health:
The community remains moderate and code-review oriented. Public dev/users
mailing-list traffic is not high, but review and maintenance work continues
steadily around the areas that matter for the next release: UI modernization,
interpreter cleanup, security posture, and contributor onboarding.

The project is preparing for OSSCA again this year. The important part is not
only that new contributors may arrive, but that the PMC has prepared a set of
low-hanging-fruit issues for them. These issues are intentionally small and
well-scoped across documentation, UI, interpreter behavior, tests, and cleanup
work, so newcomers can make real contributions without needing to understand
the whole Zeppelin stack first.

This is meant to turn the onboarding period into a path toward sustained
contribution. The PMC is also actively inviting more contributors to grow into
committer and PMC member roles.
