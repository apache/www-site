 8. Special Orders

    A. Dissolve Legal Affairs as a Board Committee

       WHEREAS, the Legal Affairs Committee was established as an Executive Committee
       of the Board of Directors by resolution of 28 March 2007 (Special Order 6C),
       responsible for establishing and managing legal policies; and

       WHEREAS, those functions have in practice been carried out through the office
       of Vice President, Legal Affairs, and the Board deems it to be in the best
       interests of the Foundation that they continue under a clear delegation of
       authority to that office;

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the Legal Affairs Committee is hereby
       dissolved as a committee of the Board, and all actions taken by or under its
       authority are hereby ratified and affirmed; and be it further

       RESOLVED, that the office of Vice President, Legal Affairs, appointed by and
       serving at the direction of the Board, be and hereby is continued, with
       responsibility for the legal affairs of the Foundation, including
       custodianship of the Apache License and the Foundation's other licensing and
       contribution instruments, establishing and maintaining legal policy binding on
       the Foundation's projects and officers, and resolving legal questions raised
       by projects and officers, with such resolutions binding on the requesting
       party, in each case unless the Board directs otherwise. The legal affairs of
       the Foundation are any matter concerning the licensing, provenance, or terms
       governing contributions to and releases of the Foundation's projects; the
       Foundation's intellectual property; contracts and agreements concerning the
       Foundation's projects, software, or intellectual property; and the regulatory
       and statutory obligations applicable to the Foundation or its projects; and be
       it further

       RESOLVED, that guidance issued by other officers and committees within their
       own areas of responsibility shall be consistent with legal policy so
       established, with questions of interpretation of such policy resolved by the

  Security: This quarter the PMC completed and disclosed the results of a
  large, systematic security audit of the component set. 39 CVEs were
  published in July and August 2026, in the following classes:

  - Header injection (24 CVEs): components that mapped externally supplied
    message metadata into the Camel Exchange without a HeaderFilterStrategy,
    or that used Exchange header constants without the Camel prefix and
    therefore bypassed the upstream HTTP header filter. This allowed an
    untrusted client to override routing or operation headers. Affected
    components included camel-cxf, camel-solr, camel-kafka, camel-salesforce,
    camel-jira, camel-dns, camel-mongodb-gridfs, camel-nats, camel-aws2-sqs,
    camel-cometd, camel-elasticsearch-rest-client, camel-vertx-websocket,
    camel-atmosphere-websocket, camel-knative and others.
  - Unsafe Java deserialization (5 CVEs): paths reading untrusted payloads
    through ObjectInputStream with no JEP-290 ObjectInputFilter
    (camel-hazelcast, camel-pqc, camel-jms), plus a hardening of Camel's own
     default filter pattern.
  - Path traversal (3 CVEs): cloud storage download operations that built the
    local target path from the remote object name without constraining it to
    the configured directory (camel-azure-storage-blob,
    camel-azure-storage-datalake, camel-google-storage).
  - Authentication and authorization (3 CVEs): token validation gaps in
    camel-keycloak and in the platform-http-main JWT mechanism.
  - Information disclosure (2 CVEs): HTTP consumers returning full Java stack
    traces in the response body because muteException defaulted to false
    (camel-netty-http, camel-undertow).
  - Injection into back-end queries (2 CVEs): Cypher injection in camel-neo4j
    and JavaMail session property override in camel-mail.

  shipping in 4.14.9, 4.18.4 and 4.22.0. Beyond the individual fixes, the

  that they are covered by the header filter strategies.

## Otava

Otava, a command-line tool, written in Python, that detects statistically
significant changes in time-series data stored either in databases or CSV
files.

A typical use-case of Otava is as follows:

A set of performance tests is scheduled repeatedly, such as after each
commit is pushed. The resulting metrics of the test runs are stored in a
time series database (Graphite) or appended to CSV files. Otava is launched
by a Jenkins/Cron job (or an operator) to analyze the recorded metrics
regularly. Otava notifies about significant changes in recorded metrics by
outputting text reports or sending Slack notifications. Otava is capable of
finding even small, but persistent shifts in metric values, despite noise
in data. It adapts automatically to the level of noise in data and tries to
notify only about persistent, statistically significant changes, be it in
the system under test or in the environment.

Otava has been incubating since 2024-11-27. Otava entered Incubation as
Hunter.
